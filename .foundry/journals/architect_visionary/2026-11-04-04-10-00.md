# Architect Visionary Journal — 2026-11-04

## Critical Learnings & Observations
- **Monolithic Engine File Gravitational Pull**: When a parser file grows beyond 2,000 lines (such as `src/engine/saveParser/parsers/gen3.ts` at 2,769 lines), it acts as an architectural sinkhole. Even when developers create modular subdirectories (e.g., `src/engine/saveParser/gen3/weatherAnomaly/`), new routines (like NPC trades, move tutors, and condition stats) gravitate back towards the central file because of existing shared helper imports. Actively drafting structural decomposition initiatives before files reach unmanageable cognitive thresholds prevents developer velocity stagnation.
- **Facade Orchestration for Safe Migration**: In critical binary-parsing domains where dozens of upstream consumers and test suites depend on exported symbols, migrating directly to fragmented files introduces high risk of parallel merge conflicts and regression. Structuring decomposition around a high-level facade pattern (where `parsers/gen3.ts` orchestrates and re-exports from modular sub-parsers under `src/engine/saveParser/gen3/`) ensures 100% backward compatibility while isolating domain responsibilities.
- **Unblocking Downstream Workspace Extraction**: Extracting domain engines into `@dexhelper/core` (`idea-735`) or WebWorker threads (`idea-731`) requires clean separation of concerns. Pure computational modules (e.g. flash bank rotation, XOR substructure decryption) must not be entangled with game-specific event flag parsing or text conversion tables. Modularizing the Gen 3 parser creates the discrete subpath boundaries necessary for zero-DOM package exports.
